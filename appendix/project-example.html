B.1 Project title:

Treatment effect of NSAID and paracetamol pain in knee osteoarthritis.

B.2 Project supervisors:

Kristian

B.3 Number of assigned groups:

The project is open for max 3 groups.

B.4 Project description:

Chronic pain is a major problem that affects 20% of the world’s population. Knee osteoarthritis is characterized by joint degeneration, and the hallmark symptom is chronic pain.

Inflammation is believed to be one of the main drivers for pain in knee osteoarthritis \(^1\) and therefore Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs) are one of the clinical recommended treatment for knee osteoarthritis \(^2\). Analgesic effects have been widely documented even though the mechanisms of action are not completely understood \(^3\). Both NSAIDs and paracetamol inhibit the synthesis of prostaglandins modulated through cyclooxygenase (COX). In the rat, non-selective NSAIDs and paracetamol enhance the activity of the cannabinoid system \(^4\), and studies suggest that the analgesic effect of NSAIDs and selective COX-2 inhibitors is dependent on an intact serotonin system 3. Further, COX-2 inhibitors modulate widespread hyperalgesia \(^{5,6}\) suggesting that NSAID treatment may also modulate central pain pathways and that mechanistic pain profiling could identify responders to NSAID treatment. NSAIDs may be potentially harmful \(^6\) and therefore, it is ideal to identify patients benefiting from NSAIDs before administrating the drug.

Pro-inflammatory cytokines are known to sensitize the peripheral nerve endings leading to hyperalgesia \(^7\), which can be assessed as lowered pressure pain thresholds (PPTs). A prolonged intense painful input from the periphery may sensitize the dorsal horn neurons leading to an increased central gain of pain \(^8\). This phenomenon can be assessed as facilitated temporal summation of pain (TSP) \(^9\). Widespread hyperalgesia can in part be explained by impaired descending pain control which is considered the net effect of pain inhibitory and facilitatory pathways \(^{10,11}\). NSAIDs has the potential to lower the inflammation and thereby modulating the pain sensitivity measures. Psychological factors should also be considered, as approximately one-fifth of patients with osteoarthritis experience symptoms of anxiety and depression \(^12\) and a large population-based study across \(^17\) diverse countries found anxiety and mood disorders to be twice as prevalent in patients with arthritis compared to those without \(^13\). Self-reported symptoms of depression and anxiety have previously been correlated to pain in osteoarthritis intensity \(^{14,15}\) and are suggested to be predictive for treatment responses \(^16\).

Assignment

In this study, you are to evaluate if a 3-weeks intervention with NSAID and paracetamol is effective and identify patients who are more likely to respond to therapy.

Data from 100 patients with data before and after therapy is available. The following parameters are available:

Treatment

Patients were administered 400 mg ibuprofen three times per day and 1g paracetamol three times per day for a three-week duration. Patients were also administered 20 mg of pantoprazole once per day to prevent ulcers. All patients were instructed to report any adverse and severe adverse events.
